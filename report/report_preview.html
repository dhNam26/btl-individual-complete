Adversarial Search and Monte Carlo Tree Search with UCT:
Theoretical Foundations, Formula Variants, and Applications to Tic-Tac-Toe, Gomoku, Xiangqi, and Delivery Scheduling
Student Author — Artificial Intelligence Midterm Project Report (2026–2027)
Abstract—This paper implements, analyzes, and empirically evaluates Minimax with Alpha–Beta pruning and Monte Carlo Tree Search with Upper Confidence Bound applied to Trees (MCTS–UCT) across 3×3 Tic-Tac-Toe, 5-in-a-row Gomoku, 9×10 Xiangqi (Chinese Chess), and real-world last-mile delivery routing with time windows (TSP-TW). We derive UCT from Hoeffding's inequality and Multi-Armed Bandit theory, explaining the square root and logarithm, and benchmark seven UCT variants (including UCB1-Tuned and heuristic-prior PUCT) alongside random, tactical heuristic, and value-cutoff rollouts. Empirically, ordered Alpha–Beta reduces visited nodes by 98.57% on 3×3 Tic-Tac-Toe (99.61% with transposition tables), 93.29% on 9×9 Gomoku, and 93.44% on Xiangqi while preserving exact minimax values. On 3×3 Tic-Tac-Toe, MCTS–UCT achieves 0% losses across 100 alternating games against optimal Alpha–Beta starting at 500 iterations. On 9×9 Gomoku, value-cutoff rollouts lift win rate from 16.0% (random) and 34.0% (heuristic) to 74.0% while running 6.2× faster than full heuristic playouts; PUCT attains 98.0% win rate. On TSP-TW, heuristic MCTS achieves a 4.78% optimality gap against exact Branch-and-Bound and lowers cost by 43.6% over nearest-neighbor greedy at 16 orders.
Index Terms—Minimax, Alpha–Beta pruning, Monte Carlo Tree Search, UCT, Multi-Armed Bandits, Gomoku, Xiangqi, Delivery Routing.

I. Introduction

Classical adversarial search relies on Minimax with Alpha–Beta pruning [2], yielding provably optimal play when terminal states are reachable. As branching factors grow from 3×3 Tic-Tac-Toe to 5-in-a-row Gomoku and 9×10 Xiangqi, exhaustive search becomes intractable. Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with UCT [4], [5] addresses this by combining selective tree expansion with stochastic rollouts modeled as Multi-Armed Bandits [3].

This study answers six research questions: RQ1 (Alpha–Beta pruning & move ordering reductions), RQ2 (iteration threshold for 0% loss on 3×3 TTT), RQ3 (interaction of exploration constant c ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.4, 2.0} and iterations {100…5000}), RQ4 (why Robust Child max N outperforms Max Child max W/N), RQ5 (UCT ablations, UCB1-Tuned/PUCT, and heuristic/value-cutoff rollouts), and RQ6 (transfer to Xiangqi and real-world delivery routing).

II. Problem Formulation

A finite two-player zero-sum perfect-information game is defined as G = (S, A, T, s0, Z, u), with states s = (B, p), active player p ∈ {+1, −1}, legal actions A(s), transition T(s, a), terminal set Z, and zero-sum payoff u ∈ {−1, 0, +1} mapped to MCTS rewards r ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}.

  • 3×3 Tic-Tac-Toe: 3 aligned marks across 8 lines; exact value V*(s0) = 0 (forced draw).
  • 5-in-a-Row Gomoku: n×n board (n ∈ {7, 9}); 5 consecutive marks along 4 directions {(0,1), (1,0), (1,1), (1,−1)}; candidate moves within Chebyshev radius r ≤ 2.
  • Xiangqi (9×10 Bonus): Full official rules including palaces, river, Flying General prohibition, Horse-leg blocking, Elephant-eye blocking, Cannon screen-jumping captures, strict check legality, and stalemate-as-loss (44 opening Red moves).
  • TSP-TW Delivery Routing (Bonus Task 2): Single courier departing depot at t=0 to deliver M orders with time windows [ej, lj], minimizing J(π) = Distance(π) + 4.0 · Σ max(0, aj − lj).

III. Methodology

A. Minimax, Alpha–Beta Pruning & Evaluation

Minimax alternates MAX and MIN levels; Alpha–Beta tracks guaranteed lower bound α and upper bound β, pruning whenever α ≥ β without altering the exact minimax value. Complexity drops from O(bd) to O(bd/2) under strong move ordering: center/corners for TTT; offensive+defensive open-line pattern scores (live-4 = 100,000, half-open-4 = 12,000, live-3 = 8,000) for Gomoku; Material + Piece-Square Tables + MVV-LVA capture ordering for Xiangqi.

B. MCTS-UCT Mathematical Foundations (Bonus)

At each node, selecting among K child moves mirrors a stochastic K-armed bandit with bounded rewards X ∈ [0, 1]. By Hoeffding's Inequality:

P(μi − X̄i ≥ ε) ≤ exp(−2 Ni ε2)

Setting failure probability δ = Np−4 (so Σ t−4 < ∞) and solving for ε yields:

ε = √(2 ln(Np) / Ni) ⇒ UCTi = Wi/Ni + c · √(ln(Np) / Ni)

This explains both operators: (1) √· reflects the O(Ni−1/2) decay of standard error; removing it explodes exploration on newly sampled nodes; (2) ln Np grows slowly to ∞, guaranteeing no arm is permanently starved while limiting suboptimal pulls to O(ln T) logarithmic regret [3], [4].

We evaluate 7 selection rules: Greedy (c=0), Linear (Q+c/N), No-Ln (Q+c√(1/N)), No-Sqrt (Q+c ln(Np)/N), Standard UCT, variance-aware UCB1-Tuned [3], and heuristic-prior PUCT (Q + c P(s,a)√Np/(1+Ni)) [7].

C. Simulation Policies

We compare (1) Random Rollout, (2) Tactical Heuristic Rollout (immediate win, immediate opponent-win block, live threats/captures), and (3) Value-Cutoff Simulation (L=4 tactical plies followed by logistic-squashed heuristic evaluation σ(Eval(s)/τ)), directly mirroring AlphaZero's transition from random playouts to a neural Value Network [6], [7].

IV. Experimental Setup

All 8 experiments ran in Python 3.13.7 on Windows 11 (16-core AMD64, 14 parallel workers) with fixed deterministic seeds. Every tournament plays ≥50 games (100 games for 3×3 TTT) strictly alternating first/second player. Benchmarks span 3×3 TTT, 7×7 & 9×9 Gomoku, 9×10 Xiangqi, and 90 TSP-TW delivery instances (30 each for M ∈ {8, 12, 16}).

VI. Discussion

Why max Ni beats max Wi/Ni: Small-sample moves (N=1–2) can display 100% win rates solely from rollout luck with huge standard error (∼0.5/√N). Visit count Ni integrates sustained adversarial survival under UCT: a move visited 795 times has survived hundreds of opponent refutations.

Precursor to Value & Policy Networks: Replacing noisy full-game playouts with a 4-ply Value-Cutoff estimator boosted 9×9 Gomoku win rate from 16% to 74% (and Xiangqi vs. Random from 66% to 96% at 8.4× speedup), while adding a heuristic PUCT prior reached 98%. Replacing these hand-crafted estimators with deep neural Value and Policy Networks is precisely the breakthrough behind AlphaGo Zero and AlphaZero [6], [7].

Limitations: Pure Python implementation limits Alpha–Beta depth and MCTS throughput compared to C++; Gomoku candidate radius r=2 is a heuristic reduction; Xiangqi omits Asian perpetual-chase federation rules.

VII. Conclusion

We verified Minimax + Alpha–Beta pruning and MCTS-UCT rigorously across 3×3 Tic-Tac-Toe, 5-in-a-row Gomoku, 9×10 Xiangqi, and real-world delivery scheduling. Hoeffding's inequality explains UCT's square-root and logarithmic structure, while extensive benchmarks demonstrate up to 99.61% pruning, 0% losses on 3×3 TTT, finite-budget tuning of c, dramatic gains from UCB1-Tuned/PUCT and Value-Cutoff rollouts, and 43.6% cost reduction in last-mile logistics.
